Privacy policy

  • Last updated: 26 October 2026
  • Version 1.0
  • 18-minute read

In short

Your check-ins, your periods, your journal, your medications and, if you use them, your Baby project or IVF tracking are recorded in a local database, on your phone. Lotte has no server and asks for no account: the publisher has no technical access to your tracking. That database is part of your phone’s iCloud or Google backup, on your own account. To work and to be discovered, the app contacts the subscription service, a usage measurement tool installed on the publisher’s own server, a crash reporting tool and the ad networks that measure campaigns (Meta, TikTok, Apple): they receive events and technical identifiers, never the content of your tracking. You can turn usage measurement off, export your data or erase everything from Settings, without asking us.

2. The principle: everything stays on your phone

Lotte is built to work without the publisher ever seeing your data. That is not a marketing promise, it is an architectural constraint:

  • What you enter is written to a local database (SQLite) and, for voice notes, to audio files, stored in the app’s private space on your device.
  • No account is created. No email address, no phone number and no password is asked for. The first name Lotte asks for during onboarding — a nickname works just as well — stays on your phone.
  • There is no Lotte server that could receive your tracking: the publisher has no technical access to it and therefore cannot read it, pass it on or sell it.
  • Logging your evening check-in, looking at your insights, writing in your journal or generating your doctor report all work without a network connection.
  • What the app sends out is limited to usage events, crash reports, and subscription and ad attribution information, detailed in sections 8 and 9. The tables that hold your tracking have no network path in the code.

4. Private journal, voice notes and lock

  • Your journal is private: its pages do not appear in your doctor report, except the ones you choose yourself to mark “Include in my doctor report”.
  • A voice note is recorded on your phone as an audio file. The microphone is only requested when you start recording, and only used while you record. Lotte does not transcribe your voice and sends your notes to no service.
  • By default, the journal is locked with Face ID, Touch ID or your phone’s passcode — on Android, with your face, your fingerprint or your device PIN. It locks again as soon as you leave the app, and removing the lock requires you to authenticate. Lotte never receives your face or your fingerprint: the system only answers “yes” or “no”.
  • The lock protects access to the journal screen; it does not replace your phone’s own lock. If your device has no passcode at all, the journal opens without asking.

5. Health data, Apple Health and Health Connect

If you explicitly allow it, Lotte connects to your phone’s health app — Apple Health (HealthKit) on iPhone, Health Connect on Android. This is optional, and done from Settings › My tracking.

  • What Lotte reads: last night’s sleep, to fill in the sleep question of your evening check-in. Lotte keeps the duration, with the check-in.
  • What Lotte writes: one thing only, the period days you log in Lotte, added to your health app’s cycle tracking. If you remove a period day in Lotte, it also removes the one it had written. Nothing else is ever written there — no pain, no symptom, no journal, no medication.

This data is used only on your phone. It is never passed on to the publisher or to any third party, never sold, never used for advertising and never used to train a model.

You can withdraw this permission at any time:

  • on iPhone, from Settings › Privacy & Security › Health › Lotte;
  • on Android, from the Health Connect app › App permissions › Lotte.

The app then keeps working with manual entry. Erasing your data in Lotte does not remove the period days already written to Apple Health or Health Connect: they belong to you and are managed from those apps.

6. iCloud backup and Google backup

Lotte has no account, so your phone’s backup is the only way to get your tracking back on a new device. That is why Lotte’s database and your voice notes are part of your phone’s system backup — iCloud on iPhone, Google backup on Android — like the data of most apps. There is no setting in Lotte to leave them out.

That copy is made on your own Apple or Google account, encrypted by them, and is a matter between you and Apple or Google: the publisher has no access to it and does not even know whether it exists. If you do not want Lotte in it, that is decided in your phone’s settings: on iPhone, the list of apps in iCloud Backup lets you leave Lotte out on its own; on Android, only the Google backup of the whole device can be turned off.

On Android, Google caps the size of each app’s backup: with many voice notes, Lotte’s backup may stop happening. The export described in section 7 is then the only safe copy.

After you erase your data in the app, a copy may remain in your backup until your phone’s next backup, and restoring an older backup brings it back. That is a limit of iOS and Android: Lotte cannot touch backups that have already been made.

7. Your doctor report and your exports

  • The doctor report (PDF) is built on your phone, with what you choose to put in it: the period covered, the sections, your doctor’s name if you add it, and only the journal pages you have marked.
  • The CSV export, from Settings › Data and privacy › Export my data, gathers your raw data in a file, also built on your phone.
  • Neither leaves on its own: they only leave your phone if you send them yourself, through the system share sheet, to the app or person you choose (messaging, email, AirDrop…). Once sent, the file is in the hands of that recipient and of the service used.
  • The last report and the export files are deleted when you erase your data.

9. Advertising, usage measurement and consent

No outside tool is started before you have made a choice. In practice:

  • At the very first launch, a consent screen appears right after the welcome screen: there you agree to Lotte processing your health data on your phone, and you accept these terms and this policy. At that point, no outside service has been initialised.
  • Ad attribution (Meta, TikTok, Apple Ads) starts once that screen is behind you. On iPhone, later in onboarding, Lotte explains and then shows iOS’s “App Tracking Transparency” prompt. Your answer only decides how Meta and TikTok measure: if you accept, with the device’s advertising identifier; if you decline — and until you have answered —, without it, in aggregate. Apple Ads always measures without that identifier. You can change your mind at any time in Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking on your iPhone.
  • On Android, that prompt does not exist: the advertising ID is used unless you have deleted it in your phone’s settings (under “Ads”). There is no setting in Lotte that turns ad measurement off.
  • Usage measurement is on by default and can be turned off in Settings › Preferences › “Anonymous usage stats”. It relies on a pseudonymous identifier: a number drawn at random on first send, which comes neither from your phone, nor from an account, nor from your IP address — which is not kept. It serves one purpose only, knowing whether the same person comes back from one day to the next, and it is used by no other app. It is stored in Lotte’s database: it disappears if you uninstall the app, and comes back if you restore a backup of your phone. Turning measurement off, or erasing your data, deletes it with immediate effect; turn it back on and you start again with a new one.
  • Events describe the action, never its content: “check-in saved” is sent with no pain level, no body area, no mood and no day. The full list of possible events is: each onboarding step reached and its completion, your answer to the tracking prompt (accepted or declined), the subscription page being opened and where it was opened from, the plan chosen (annual or weekly), a purchase completed, cancelled or failed and its amount, a purchase restored, the reason for cancelling chosen from a fixed list, the loyalty offer viewed or accepted, a check-in saved (and whether it is the first), SOS mode opened and where from, the heat timer, the positions, a flare-up logged, the flare-up plan saved, a period logged, a journal page saved (written or voice), the triggers viewed, a report generated or shared, an export (PDF or CSV), data erased, a cover changed or unlocked, a treatment added, Baby project turned on and its mode (trying naturally, in the coming months or IVF), the IVF space created and pregnancy mode turned on. On top of that come the screens opened, identified by their generic name (for instance “journal-entry/[id]”), never by an identifier or a date.
  • You should still know this: some of these names, on their own, say something about how you use the app — for instance that an IVF screen was opened, that pregnancy mode was turned on, or that a period was logged on a given day, since each event is timestamped when it is sent. That is why they are tied only to a randomly drawn identifier, which the publisher cannot link to anyone, and why you can turn measurement off at any time.
  • Crash reports follow the same switch as usage measurement: when it is off, no crash is sent. Until consent has been given, the tool is not even started: a crash that happens before that moment is never known to the publisher.
  • Declining ad tracking or turning usage measurement off does not degrade any feature and triggers no reminder. It is a stable state.

10. Notifications

The evening check-in reminder, the journal page (if you turn it on), your upcoming period, the fertile window (Baby project), medication and appointment reminders, new insights and the end of the free trial are local notifications, scheduled by your phone from your own data. No sending server is involved, and no notification token is passed to anyone.

Depending on your phone’s settings, their text may show on the lock screen — for instance “Your period is due in 2 days” or the name of a medication. You choose which ones you get in Settings › Reminders, and you can hide their preview or turn them off in your phone’s settings. The end-of-trial reminder is always sent, so that no charge takes you by surprise, unless Lotte’s notifications are turned off on your phone.

12. Retention periods

  • The data you enter stays on your device for as long as the app is installed on it. Uninstalling deletes the local database and the voice notes; copies made in your phone’s backup follow Apple’s or Google’s rules.
  • Usage measurement events are kept for twelve months at most, then deleted. The pseudonymous identifier that links them disappears from your phone as soon as you turn measurement off or erase your data: events already received can then no longer be linked to anyone.
  • Crash reports are kept for thirty days by Sentry, then deleted.
  • Subscription data is kept by Apple or Google and by RevenueCat for as long as their accounting and tax obligations require.
  • Attribution events received by Meta, TikTok and Apple are kept under their own policies, as are GitHub’s technical logs for this site.

13. Your rights, and how to exercise them

Since the publisher has no access to your tracking, most of your rights are exercised directly in the app. It is quicker, and it avoids having to send us data just to have it deleted.

Access and portability Settings › Data and privacy › Export my data. You get a readable PDF or a CSV file with your check-ins, periods, flare-ups, the written pages of your journal, your medications and doses, your Baby project tracking, your IVF journey and your appointments. 14. Minors

The app is not intended for people under sixteen. No data is knowingly collected from a minor under sixteen without the permission of the holder of parental authority. The age range you give during onboarding stays on your phone: the publisher does not receive it. If you find that this has happened, write to timotheduc+lotte@gmail.com.

15. Transfers outside the European Union

Usage measurement (OpenPanel) runs on the publisher’s server, in France. Crash reports are stored in Sentry’s European region, in Frankfurt; the company itself is based in the United States, and any access by its staff relies on the standard contractual clauses adopted by the European Commission. RevenueCat is based in the United States: the transfer relies on the same standard contractual clauses, and is limited to the subscription information and technical identifiers described in section 8. No health data is involved in these transfers.

Meta, TikTok and Apple receive the attribution events described above under their own transfer safeguards. This site is hosted by GitHub, a US company that states it participates in the EU–US Data Privacy Framework: only your IP address, at the moment you view a page, is involved.

16. Security

  • The database and the voice notes are stored in the app’s private space, protected by the device’s encryption and the system sandbox. No other app, widget or extension has access to it.
  • The private journal is locked by default (section 4).
  • All network communication uses HTTPS.
  • No table holding health data has a network path in the code, and crash reports are filtered before sending (section 8).
  • Your phone remains the first line of protection: your health data is stored on it, and anyone who unlocks it can open Lotte. A passcode is strongly recommended.
